## The sketch hand

At the owner's ask ("for the countdown of play button, focus countdown, it could be more sketchy ... like creative sketch, not too many, not too little, just simple, and adds a twist"), clarified as "it's just not specific for countdown, it's specific for the whole app".

The idea came from the method rather than from a style: WilList is Tracy's written list, where you letter each task with a pen and circle the one that comes first.
So the marks that say "this one" are now drawn rather than set.
The twist is that one pen circle carries that meaning everywhere: the check you have ticked, the focus ring you are inside, the tab you are in, and the A1.

`ios/WilList/Sketch.swift` holds the whole language in three shapes and one colour: `SketchCircle` (ten hand-set points run through a smooth curve, starting at the top and going clockwise so a trim from 0 shows time; an overshoot carries the pen past its own start and crosses it), `SketchTick`, `SketchRule`, and `Color.pencil`.
The wobble is hand-set rather than random, so every mark in the app is the same pen and nothing shimmers when a list is laid out again.

Used in five places and no more:

- Every circle you tick or choose, through one view (`DrawnCheck`): every row's check on every tab, a step in a task's details, Today's add bar once a line is being written, and the guided flows' written lines and "Which one first?" rows.
  Where a circle is only ever empty it is `DrawnCheck(done: false)`, so the app has one circle and not two.
- The focus ring, with no overshoot (a quarter drawn must be a quarter of the time), and the time left moved to the system's rounded face, which sits with a drawn ring where the plain one did not.
- The chosen tab's symbol, circled in the deep teal in place of the pale teal pill.
- The A1's letter on Today and in Plan, circled in graphite.
- A rule under the day's date on Today and Plan.

Three things found by looking at the render rather than the code:

- A filled sketch circle with no stroke reads as a blob, not as a drawn mark; keeping the pen edge when it fills (and the little tail past the top) fixed it.
- A closed circle whose curve reads its neighbours only within the array leaves a sharp peak where the pen meets its start, plain at Break where the ring is full.
  The closed variant now reads its neighbours round the loop (`wrap`).
- The first ring round a tab's symbol was 36 by 30 and cut through the wider symbols; 42 by 32 goes round them.

Motion is unchanged in kind: the tick is trimmed by whether the row is done, so a tap draws it on while a row that is already done, scrolled into view, simply has it.
Nothing draws itself on arrival, and Reduce Motion is respected as before.
Deliberately left alone: the letters' chips, the star, the course colours, the charts, and the widgets (pitfall 13).
The Agent's find rows still tick with the system's circles, as this session was told not to touch the Agent; that is the one place the language stops short.

Checked: 77 core tests, lint, a zero-warning release build, the UI tests, and every changed screen looked at in the simulator (Today, Plan, Progress, Goals, the add bar mid-line, a row the moment it is ticked, Focus part-way and at Break, and Focus at Display Zoom's 320 points).

## The hand goes everywhere (2026-10-29)

The owner saw the five drawn marks on every tab and asked for the rest of it: "would be better if the text and icons are all sketch, human sketch, not generic sketch".
So the restraint of the pass above, which was deliberate for marks, is lifted for type and icons: every word in the app is now written in one hand, and every icon WilList lays out itself is drawn by the pen that draws the circles.
The twist is untouched: a pen circle still means "this one", and no new circle was added anywhere.

### Picking the hand

Six faces went on screen side by side, at the app's own sizes and colours, before one was chosen: Noteworthy (Light and Bold), Bradley Hand, Marker Felt (Thin and Wide), Chalkboard SE, Caveat and Architects Daughter.

- Marker Felt and Chalkboard SE read as a sketch effect rather than as a person, which is the thing the owner asked against.
- Noteworthy is Apple's own notes face and reads as a font.
- Caveat registers only `Caveat-Regular` from its variable file on iOS, and it is too light and too small in the eye at footnote size.
- Bradley Hand is genuinely a person's pen and ships with iOS, so it was the one to beat. It lost on the line that matters most: the quiet grey line under a row, carrying "11/9", "9:00 AM", "14:25" and "1 of 3", where its strokes go spindly.
- **Patrick Hand** is the same kind of human hand with an even, confident stroke that stays crisp in grey at 13 points, so it was worth bundling a file for.

It is bundled as `ios/WilList/PatrickHand-Regular.ttf` under the SIL Open Font License (`PatrickHand-OFL.txt` beside it), registered as `UIAppFonts` in `Info.plist`, and reached only through `Font.hand(_:size:)` in `Sketch.swift`.

Three things fell out of the choice:

- `Font.hand` always builds with `relativeTo:`, so every size still grows with Dynamic Type. `Font.handSize` holds the system's own point size per text style, because `UIFont.preferredFont` hands back a size that is already scaled and `relativeTo:` would scale it a second time.
- **A hand has one pen, so there is no bold.** The family has one weight and SwiftUI will not synthesise another, so every `.weight(.semibold)` and `.bold()` went; emphasis is size and colour now, which is what a person writing actually has. Nothing on screen lost its hierarchy: the tabs already lean on teal, the letters on their five colours, and the quiet lines on grey.
- There is no monospaced face either, but the hand's digits are near enough even that the focus clock does not jump as it counts down.

The root sets `\.font` to the hand, so everything that asks for no face of its own gets it; about 70 explicit `.font(...)` calls were rewritten; and `UINavigationBar.appearance()` carries it into the large title and the small one, which SwiftUI does not reach.
One file in `ios/Shared` moved for this: `LetterMark` now reads `\.markFont` from the environment and falls back to the system's type, so the app writes its letters in the hand and the widgets, which leave it nil, are unchanged.

### The icons

`SketchIcon` draws thirty glyphs as strokes through hand-set points in the unit square, run through the same `sketched()` curve as the circles and the tick, with the wobble written down rather than random so a list of rows never shimmers as it is laid out again.
One stroke style everywhere: round caps and joins, `side / 12.5` wide (about 2 pt at 24 pt), in the colour of wherever it sits, scaling with Dynamic Type.

Drawn and in use: the five tab icons, play, pause, star, magnifier, plus, plusCircle, sort, chevron, check, xmark, paperclip, page, photo, trash, restore, share, bell, calendar, repeatArrows, sun, person, pencil, cap, warning, flag, target, bars, checklist, listNumber, sparkles.

Four were redrawn after looking at them on screen rather than in the source:

- The **paperclip** first came out as a plain capsule, because its three vertical runs sat closer together than the stroke was wide.
- **Restore** read as an apple: its arrowhead stood up like a stem, so the arc was given a gap and the head turned to lie along it.
- **Share** read as a shopping bag until its box got vertical sides.
- The **flag** read as a letter P until its cloth got bigger.

Two were drawn against the twist rather than for it.
The **Goals target** is one ring with a dart in it, not rings inside rings: under the tab bar's own pen circle a bullseye turned to soup, and a second circle there would blunt what that circle means.
The **play** mark is drawn rather than filled in, so it reads as the same pen as everything beside it.

The few marks that sit inside a run of words (the bell, calendar, repeat and page on a to-do's line) cannot be views, since `Text` will only take an `Image`: `SketchIcon.inline` draws each one once through `ImageRenderer`, keeps it, and hands it over as a template image so the words' own colour still tints it.

### What stayed the system's

Anything WilList does not lay out itself and cannot restyle: menu popovers, swipe actions, `ContentUnavailableView`'s own symbol, the keyboard, the share sheet, Sign in with Apple, `PhotosPicker`, the file importer, steppers and toggles, and the symbols the system's own `TabView` and the App Shortcuts are given.
The widgets keep SF Symbols and the system's type throughout (pitfall 13).

### Two things only the simulator showed

- A drawn icon carries no name, so `.labelStyle(.iconOnly)` on a sketch `Label` left a button with no description at all, where `Button(_, systemImage:)` used to leave the symbol's. The accessibility audit caught five of them (the month arrows in Plan's calendar, Back in the guided flows). Every icon-only control now says what it is on `.accessibilityLabel`.
- iOS 26's glass toolbar buttons draw the *words* of a `Label` whose icon is not an `Image`, so Search, Sort and view, Add and Clear came back as text in the bar. They take the drawn icon directly now, with their name on `.accessibilityLabel`.
