# The principles behind WilList

WilList is built on Brian Tracy's *Phoenix Seminar on the Psychology of Achievement*
(the transcript is in [`briantracy.md`](../briantracy.md)). This file maps every idea
that matters for a student's workload to the place in the app that applies it.
"S17" means session 17 of the seminar.

The transcript contains sessions 1–17, 19, 20 and 24–27. Sessions 18 and 21–23 are
missing from it. Two ideas come from Tracy's other time-management teaching, not from
this transcript. They are marked **(outside the transcript)**.

## The core loop

| Principle | Where it comes from | In WilList |
|---|---|---|
| **The law of control**: you feel good about yourself to the degree you feel in control of your life. Time management *is* "control of the sequence of events". | S2, S17 | The whole app. One place for classes, deadlines, exams, readings and errands, plus a 14-day Radar. |
| **Think on paper**: get it all out of your head onto a list. | S17, S20 | The + button (or `/`) on every screen opens *Write it down*, in plain language: `Chem quiz thu 2pm #CHEM110`. Plan → "Empty your head" takes many lines at once. Tools → Bring in your semester reads a whole LMS calendar or a pasted syllabus in one step. |
| **ABCDE**: A = must do, B = should do, C = nice to do, D = delegate, E = eliminate. Number your A's (A1, A2…). Never work on an A2 or a B while A1 is undone. | S17 | Every item gets a letter. Today numbers them A1, A2, B1…, and Plan → "Number your A's" reorders them. B's are dimmed while any A is open, and starting a B while A's remain asks "are you sure?". |
| **Consider consequences**: an A is anything with serious consequences if you don't do it. | *Eat That Frog!* **(outside the transcript)** | The sorter shows one item at a time and asks one question: *"If you don't do it, what happens?"* For coursework the consequence is measurable: the share of the grade (`25%`) pencils in the letter. 10% or more suggests an A, 3% or more a B. |
| **Urgent vs. important**: "urgent things are seldom important; important things are seldom urgent." | **(outside the transcript)** | The list is ordered by letter, not by due date. A C due today sits below an A due next week. Start dates bring important work onto Today before it turns urgent, and the Weekly review has an "Important, not urgent" section. |
| **What is the most valuable use of my time right now?** | S17 | Written in pen above your A1 every day. |
| **80/20, the vital few vs. the trivial many** | S17 | Radar → "The vital few" (your A/B exams, presentations, deadlines and readings with countdowns and progress bars). A nudge appears when more than five things are marked A. Grade weights make the vital few visible: a handful of items carry most of the grade. |
| **Make tomorrow's list the night before**; the subconscious works on it while you sleep. | S17 | Plan → Plan tomorrow: empty your head, letter everything, look at tomorrow, number your A's, lock it in. An evening prompt appears after your plan time. |
| **Every minute spent planning saves 5–15 minutes in execution.** | S17 | The morning prompt shown when you skipped last night's plan. |
| **Failing to plan is planning to fail** (the law of accident). | S2 | Plan tab header and the weekly review. |
| **Time management is control of the sequence of events.** | S17 | Today → My day lays out the sequence: your A1 in the first free gap between classes, then A2, then the B's, with breaks. What doesn't fit is listed so you choose what to drop. |

## Getting it done

| Principle | Source | In WilList |
|---|---|---|
| **Concentration of power / single handling**: first things first, one at a time, stay until 100% complete. | S17, S20 | Focus mode: one task, one timer, nothing else. |
| **Do it now; sense of urgency; fast tempo.** Finishing gives a jolt of energy and self-esteem. | S17 | "Start now" on the A1 card; done messages rotate Tracy lines. |
| **Take action, launch.** The first 20% of a job accounts for 80% of its value; well begun is half done. | S20 | Every planned item has a start date that puts its first session on Today well before the due date. |
| **Set a deadline, then many sub-deadlines; establish a reward schedule.** | S16 | Every exam, presentation, assignment and reading is split into dated sessions (sub-deadlines). Settings → "Reward for finishing your A's" shows on Today. |
| **Inspect what you expect**: delegation is not abdication. | S20 | D items stay visible with who you delegated to, labelled "follow up". |
| **Act your way into feeling.** | S10 | Focus mode tip; starting is one tap. |
| **Discipline**: do what you should, when you should, whether you feel like it or not. | S9 | Done messages. |
| **A new habit takes about 21 days of repetition.** | S9 | The A1-first streak on Today counts the days in a row where the first thing you finished was an A, and shows the last seven. |
| **Feeling listless? Make a list.** | S19 | The whole capture-first design. |
| **Everything counts**: nothing is neutral; it moves you toward your goals or away. | S9, S11 | E = Eliminate; the weekly review asks whether C/D/E items older than two weeks are still worth it. |

## Studying (rapid learning)

| Principle | Source | In WilList |
|---|---|---|
| **Cram-and-dump is lost within weeks; use spaced repetition.** | S13 | Exam prep is spread over expanding gaps before the exam (1, 2, 4, 7, 11… days out), ending with a light review the day before. |
| **You remember the beginning and end of a study period best: take breaks.** | S13 | Sessions default to ~50 minutes; focus mode tells you to take a break at the end. |
| **Define why you're learning it; intense desire; variety.** | S13 | The first exam session is "list every topic, mark the gaps". |
| **You become what you teach.** | S11 | Recall sessions say "test yourself, no notes". |
| **Read one hour a day.** | S11 | Readings get their own steady sessions and a start date. |
| **Early to bed, early to rise; the golden hour.** | S9, S11 | The last exam session says "sleep early". |
| **Ask your way to success.** | S16 | Goals → "Whose help do you need?" (professor, TA, advisor). The optional coach answers questions about your own week. |

## Goals

| Principle | Source | In WilList |
|---|---|---|
| **Success is goals; all else is commentary.** ~3% have written goals. | S15 | The Goals tab. |
| **Major definite purpose**: one measurable goal bigger than the rest. | S15 | Pinned at the top of Today. |
| **Seven questions** (values, three goals, six months to live, a million dollars, afraid to attempt, greatest importance, can't fail). | S15 | Goals → "Not sure what your goals are?" |
| **Balance**: personal (why), career (what), self-improvement (means), 3–5 each. | S15, S17 | Goals are grouped into these three areas, with a nudge when an area is empty. Weekly review has a balance check. |
| **90-day short-term, 2–3-year long-term goals.** | S15 | The Horizon field. |
| **12 steps**: desire, belief (≈50/50 odds, the next logical step), write it, list the reasons why, know your starting point, deadline, obstacles and "the rock", knowledge needed, whose help you need, plan, visualize, persist. | S16 | Goal fields: goal (present tense), deadline, why, the rock, knowledge, people, reward, plus a "next step" task. |
| **Keep goals confidential** except with people who have goals of their own. | S15 | Tip on the Goals tab. |
| **Your mind can work on 10–15 goals at a time.** | S12 | Nudge above 15 goals. |
| **Fail forward; persistence is your measure of belief in yourself.** | S15, S16 | Missed sessions are flagged without blame, with one-tap "Re-plan from today". |

## Mindset tools

| Principle | Source | In WilList |
|---|---|---|
| **Worry buster**: define it in writing, determine the worst possible outcome, resolve to accept it, start improving on it. Get the facts. The antidote to worry is purposeful action. ~96% of worries never matter. | S8 | Tools → Worry Buster; the actions become tasks. |
| **Quick programming and mental rehearsal before a one-off event**: visualize the perfect outcome, affirm it, feel it, release it. Recall past excellence, not past failure. | S12 | Tools → Pre-game, pre-filled with your next exam or presentation. |
| **Law of relaxation**: in mental work, effort defeats itself. | S12 | Pre-game's closing message. |
| **Mind storming**: write a goal as a question, force 20 answers, act on one immediately. | S20 | Tools → 20 Ideas; the picked idea becomes an A. |
| **Positive language**: "problem" → "situation" / "challenge". **Victim language**: "I can't", "I have to", "I'll try", "I wish". | S7, S20 | Quick-add gently flags these words as you type. |
| **I am responsible**: no blame; ask "what can we do from here?" | S5, S6 | Re-plan instead of looking back. |
| **Discard past labels**: "next time I will…". | S11 | Missed-session message. |

## WilList's own additions

These aren't from the seminar. They make the ideas above work for a student's actual week.

| Idea | Why | In WilList |
|---|---|---|
| **Learn the real pace.** | A plan built on wishful estimates fails quietly, then all at once. | Focus mode logs real minutes. After three finished sessions of a type, the weekly review shows the ratio ("Reading takes you about 1.4× the plan") and offers to plan that way from now on. |
| **Load-aware planning.** | Sub-deadlines only help if the days they land on have room. | New sessions go to days with free study time first. Radar shows each day's load against your budget, and Balance my plan gives A's first claim. |
| **Import with de-duplication.** | The semester changes; your list shouldn't fill with copies. | Re-importing adds what's new, updates changed dates and skips the rest. |
| **The coach stays in Tracy's lane.** | Advice is only useful if it's about your items. | The coach sees a summary of your planner and is told to coach in the spirit of Tracy's methods, never to claim to be him. See [AI.md](AI.md). |

## Deliberately left out

Some of the seminar doesn't belong in a planner: the law of attraction and "parking-space
programming" (S19), subliminal and taped affirmations (S14), relationships (S24–25),
raising children (S26), and the income examples. They are a big part of the seminar, but
building them in would make the app less simple, which you asked it to be.
