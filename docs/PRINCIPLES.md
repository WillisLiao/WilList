# The principles behind WilList

WilList is built on Brian Tracy's *Phoenix Seminar on the Psychology of Achievement*
(the transcript is in [`briantracy.md`](../briantracy.md)). This file maps every idea
that matters for a student's workload to the place in the app that applies it.
"S17" means session 17 of the seminar.

The transcript contains sessions 1–17, 19, 20 and 24–27. Sessions 18 and 21–23 are
missing from it. Two ideas come from Tracy's other time-management teaching, not from
this transcript. They are marked **(outside the transcript)**.

**Where to look:** the section "In the iPhone app" near the end is where each idea lives today.
The tables before it, under "On the website", record where each idea lived in the website, which was taken out of the code on 2026-10-24 at the owner's ask.
They stay as the list of every idea to keep; the ones the app doesn't have yet are listed at the end of "In the iPhone app".

## The core loop

| Principle | Where it comes from | On the website |
|---|---|---|
| **The law of control**: you feel good about yourself to the degree you feel in control of your life. Time management *is* "control of the sequence of events". | S2, S17 | The whole app. One place for classes, deadlines, exams, readings and errands. Today lays them out as one day in time order; Ahead shows the next two weeks. |
| **Think on paper**: get it all out of your head onto a list. | S17, S20 | The + button (or `/`) on every screen opens *Write it down*, in plain language: `Chem quiz thu 2pm #CHEM110`. The sheet stays open so you can empty your head in one go. More → Import semester reads a whole LMS calendar or a pasted syllabus in one step. |
| **ABCDE**: A = must do, B = should do, C = nice to do, D = delegate, E = eliminate. Number your A's (A1, A2…). Never work on an A2 or a B while A1 is undone. | S17 | Every item gets a letter. Today numbers them A1, A2, B1…, and Today → Tomorrow reorders them. B's are dimmed while any A is open, and starting a B while A's remain asks first. |
| **Consider consequences**: an A is anything with serious consequences if you don't do it. | *Eat That Frog!* **(outside the transcript)** | The sorter shows one item at a time and asks one question: *"If you don't do it, what happens?"* For coursework the consequence is measurable: the share of the grade (`25%`) pencils in the letter. 10% or more suggests an A, 3% or more a B. |
| **Urgent vs. important**: "urgent things are seldom important; important things are seldom urgent." | **(outside the transcript)** | The list is ordered by letter, not by due date. A C due today sits below an A due next week. Start dates bring important work onto Today before it turns urgent, and Ahead offers "Do one today" on A's that are weeks away. |
| **What is the most valuable use of my time right now?** | S17 | Your A1 is the first row on Today, with Start beside it and its check to tick it off. |
| **80/20, the vital few vs. the trivial many** | S17 | Ahead draws each A due in the next two weeks as a peak, as high as its share of the grade, filled in as far as you have studied. "Coming up" lists the A's and B's with countdowns and progress. A note appears when more than five things are marked A. Grade weights make the vital few visible. |
| **Make tomorrow's list the night before**; the subconscious works on it while you sleep. | S17 | Today → Tomorrow: tomorrow's day laid out, A's numbered by moving them, then Lock in. The greeting says "Plan it tonight" until you do, and a "Plan tomorrow" nudge appears after your plan time. |
| **Every minute spent planning saves 5–15 minutes in execution.** | S17 | The evening nudge: two minutes, then lock it in. |
| **Failing to plan is planning to fail** (the law of accident). | S2 | Tomorrow's "Lock in" step. |
| **Time management is control of the sequence of events.** | S17 | Today is your day in time order: classes as fixed blocks, your A1 in the first free gap, then A2, then the B's, with breaks and free time shown. What doesn't fit hangs below the end-of-day line, and the greeting says how much too much, so you choose what to move or drop. |

## Getting it done

| Principle | Source | On the website |
|---|---|---|
| **Concentration of power / single handling**: first things first, one at a time, stay until 100% complete. | S17, S20 | Focus mode: one task, one timer, nothing else. |
| **Do it now; sense of urgency; fast tempo.** Finishing gives a jolt of energy and self-esteem. | S17 | "Start" beside the A1 on Today; short done messages. |
| **Take action, launch.** The first 20% of a job accounts for 80% of its value; well begun is half done. | S20 | Every planned item has a start date that puts its first session on Today well before the due date. |
| **Set a deadline, then many sub-deadlines; establish a reward schedule.** | S16 | Every exam, presentation, assignment and reading is split into dated sessions (sub-deadlines). Goals → More → Reward. |
| **Inspect what you expect**: delegation is not abdication. | S20 | D items stay on Today, below the end of the day, with who has them. |
| **Act your way into feeling.** | S10 | Starting is one tap. |
| **Discipline**: do what you should, when you should, whether you feel like it or not. | S9 | Done messages. |
| **A new habit takes about 21 days of repetition.** | S9 | Goals shows the last 21 days as squares, filled where the first thing you finished was an A, with the current streak. |
| **Feeling listless? Make a list.** | S19 | The whole capture-first design. |
| **Everything counts**: nothing is neutral; it moves you toward your goals or away. | S9, S11 | E = Eliminate ("Drop it"); Ahead asks whether C/D/E items older than two weeks are still worth it. |

## Studying (rapid learning)

| Principle | Source | On the website |
|---|---|---|
| **Cram-and-dump is lost within weeks; use spaced repetition.** | S13 | Exam prep is spread over expanding gaps before the exam (1, 2, 4, 7, 11… days out), ending with a light review the day before. |
| **You remember the beginning and end of a study period best: take breaks.** | S13 | Sessions default to ~50 minutes; focus mode tells you to take a break at the end. |
| **Define why you're learning it; intense desire; variety.** | S13 | The first exam session is "list every topic, mark the gaps". |
| **You become what you teach.** | S11 | Recall sessions say "test yourself, no notes". |
| **Read one hour a day.** | S11 | Readings get their own steady sessions and a start date. |
| **Early to bed, early to rise; the golden hour.** | S9, S11 | The last exam session says "sleep early". |
| **Ask your way to success.** | S16 | Goals → "Whose help do you need?" (professor, TA, advisor). The optional coach answers questions about your own week. |

## Goals

| Principle | Source | On the website |
|---|---|---|
| **Success is goals; all else is commentary.** ~3% have written goals. | S15 | The Goals tab. |
| **Major definite purpose**: one measurable goal bigger than the rest. | S15 | The headline of the Goals tab. |
| **Seven questions** (values, three goals, six months to live, a million dollars, afraid to attempt, greatest importance, can't fail). | S15 | Goals → "Not sure? 7 questions". |
| **Balance**: personal (why), career (what), self-improvement (means), 3–5 each. | S15, S17 | Each goal has an area: school & career, people, health & self. |
| **90-day short-term, 2–3-year long-term goals.** | S15 | Goals → More → Horizon. |
| **12 steps**: desire, belief (≈50/50 odds, the next logical step), write it, list the reasons why, know your starting point, deadline, obstacles and "the rock", knowledge needed, whose help you need, plan, visualize, persist. | S16 | Goal fields: goal (present tense), deadline, why, the rock, knowledge, people, reward, plus a "next step" task. |
| **Keep goals confidential** except with people who have goals of their own. | S15 | Tip on the Goals tab. |
| **Your mind can work on 10–15 goals at a time.** | S12 | Nudge above 15 goals. |
| **Fail forward; persistence is your measure of belief in yourself.** | S15, S16 | Missed sessions are flagged without blame, with one-tap "Re-plan from today". |

## Mindset tools

| Principle | Source | On the website |
|---|---|---|
| **Worry buster**: define it in writing, determine the worst possible outcome, resolve to accept it, start improving on it. Get the facts. The antidote to worry is purposeful action. ~96% of worries never matter. | S8 | More → Worry Buster; the actions become tasks. |
| **Quick programming and mental rehearsal before a one-off event**: visualize the perfect outcome, affirm it, feel it, release it. Recall past excellence, not past failure. | S12 | More → Pre-game, pre-filled with your next exam or presentation. |
| **Law of relaxation**: in mental work, effort defeats itself. | S12 | Pre-game's closing message. |
| **Mind storming**: write a goal as a question, force 20 answers, act on one immediately. | S20 | More → 20 Ideas; the picked idea becomes an A. |
| **Positive language**: "problem" → "situation" / "challenge". **Victim language**: "I can't", "I have to", "I'll try", "I wish". | S7, S20 | Quick-add gently flags these words as you type. |
| **I am responsible**: no blame; ask "what can we do from here?" | S5, S6 | Re-plan instead of looking back. |
| **Discard past labels**: "next time I will…". | S11 | Missed-session message. |

## WilList's own additions

These aren't from the seminar. They make the ideas above work for a student's actual week.

| Idea | Why | On the website |
|---|---|---|
| **Learn the real pace.** | A plan built on wishful estimates fails quietly, then all at once. | Focus mode logs real minutes. After three finished sessions of a type, Ahead shows the ratio ("Reading takes you 1.4× longer") and offers to adjust. |
| **Load-aware planning.** | Sub-deadlines only help if the days they land on have room. | New sessions go to days with free study time first. The Ahead chart shows each day's load against your budget, and Balance gives A's first claim. |
| **An honest day.** | A plan that silently overflows teaches you to ignore it. | Today's greeting states whether the rest of the day fits ("Tuesday. It all fits." or "Tuesday. 45 min too much."), from your classes, your work and the time left. |
| **A quick list.** | Not everything earns a letter: sorting "buy stamps" takes longer than doing it. | The Quick tab: type it, tick it off. No letters, dates or plans, and it never reaches Today, so the A's stay first there. |
| **Import with de-duplication.** | The semester changes; your list shouldn't fill with copies. | Re-importing adds what's new, updates changed dates and skips the rest. |
| **The coach stays in Tracy's lane.** | Advice is only useful if it's about your items. | The coach sees a summary of your planner and is told to coach in the spirit of Tracy's methods, never to claim to be him. See [AI.md](AI.md). |

## In the iPhone app

The app (`ios/`) is WilList now.
It keeps the ideas above in five tabs (Today, Plan, Progress, Goals, Agent), so each place answers one question.

| Principle | In the iPhone app |
|---|---|
| **Think on paper.** | Today has a plain checklist: type a line, press return, type the next, and the keyboard stays up. Plan has **+** (a task or a study block), which reads `Chem quiz thu 2pm #CHEM110` the same way the website did and stays open for the next one. |
| **Get the whole term onto the list at once.** | The **Agent** tab reads any file, photo or words (a syllabus, a timetable, a roster, for any course, job or project) and proposes everything in it on one card to tick and Add; nothing goes in before that tap. A calendar file is read on the phone itself, and a calendar link the student types (Canvas, Moodle, Blackboard, Brightspace, Google, Outlook) can be followed, read again every half hour. |
| **ABCDE and A1 first.** | Today opens with the A1 as its first row, and the widgets show the same A1. Plan numbers the day A1, A2, B1, each letter on the right of its row. An item without a letter is fine: it sits under Later with no mark and nothing asks for one (the owner's call); its details give it one, each letter shown with what it means. |
| **Single handling: one thing until it's done.** | **Play** beside every task, on Today and in Plan, asks how long (15 minutes to an hour and a half, or the session's planned time) and opens focus: the task and its timer, nothing else. The timer also sits on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island with Mark done on it, and says Break when the planned minutes are up. The real minutes are kept, as on the website. |
| **Consider consequences.** | Tapping a letter lists all five with what each one means, and the suggested one is marked. A share of the grade suggests the letter, as on the web. |
| **Make tomorrow's list the night before.** | **Plan tomorrow** takes two short steps: write down anything new, then pick the one thing that matters most. It ends with "Tomorrow starts with" and that A1. From your planning time Today offers it at the top, and the optional evening notification opens it; Plan's calendar shows any other day too. |
| **Sub-deadlines and spaced repetition.** | Today and Plan carry today's study sessions. Progress lists each exam and deadline in the next 45 days (or with no date yet), how many sessions are done and whether it is on track, behind or short of time, and suggests replanning missed sessions or starting important work early. |
| **Load-aware planning.** | Progress charts the next 7 days' planned study per day, study blocks included, with days over your daily study time in red, and suggests what to move. |
| **Review your progress.** | Progress shows the last 7 days (done, A's, days started with an A, focus time) and a chart of the last 14 by letter; the Agent answers questions about your plan and progress in words. |
| **A new habit takes about 21 days.** | The Goals tab shows 21 dots, filled where the first thing you finished was an A, and the days in a row. |
| **Victim language.** | While you type in **+**, "I'll try", "I wish", "I have to" and "problem" get a one-line nudge. |
| **Finishing gives a jolt of energy.** | Ticking something off fills the circle with a ring and a burst of sparks, a short chime and a haptic, then the row slides away. The widgets and the Live Activity tick off too, and a widget shows the line done for a moment before it goes. |
| **Slice and dice the task.** | Every to-do and item can hold steps, ticked off one by one, with "1 of 3" in the list. |
| **Written goals, a major definite purpose.** | The Goals tab, between Progress and Agent, leads with your main goal set large on the teal, then your other goals. |
| **Plan every day in advance.** | Plan's calendar shows what's planned on every day of the month. Any to-do or item can have a reminder at a date and time, a due date and time, and a repeat; the reminder can be marked done from the notification. |

Worry Buster, 20 Ideas, Pre-game and the seven questions for finding goals stay on the website only: on 2026-10-24 the owner said the app doesn't need them, with the Goals tab back.
The Agent takes the coach's place.
Their data is kept untouched in the save file.

## Deliberately left out

Some of the seminar doesn't belong in a planner: the law of attraction and "parking-space
programming" (S19), subliminal and taped affirmations (S14), relationships (S24–25),
raising children (S26), and the income examples. They are a big part of the seminar, but
building them in would make the app less simple, which you asked it to be.
